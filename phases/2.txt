# MM Electricals — Phase 2

## Inventory, Batch Pricing & Smart Product Finder

The MM Electricals Phase 1 foundation has already been implemented.

First inspect the existing repository and architecture.

Do NOT rewrite the foundation.

This phase is:

**PHASE 2 — INVENTORY MANAGEMENT, BATCH PRICING AND SMART PRODUCT FINDER**

Do not implement the complete Sales/Purchase transaction workflow yet.

---

# 1. PRIMARY GOAL

Build a highly usable inventory system for an electrical shop.

The inventory system should not merely show stock.

It should help the owner answer:

* What products do I have?
* How much stock do I have?
* What did I pay for it?
* Which vendor supplied it?
* What did I pay last time?
* What was my best purchase price?
* What selling price did I use?
* What is the minimum safe selling price?
* How much discount can I safely give?
* Is this a new or previous batch?
* Which vendor is better?
* Which products are low stock?
* Which products are not moving?

---

# 2. PRODUCT MANAGEMENT

Implement:

* product list
* create product
* edit product
* product details
* activate/deactivate product
* categories
* subcategories
* brands
* units
* tags
* aliases
* SKU
* barcode
* HSN
* GST rate
* storage location

Use pagination and server-side filtering.

The application should comfortably support at least 50,000 products.

---

# 3. SMART PRODUCT FINDER

Build a global product search system.

The owner should be able to search using:

* partial product name
* letters
* SKU
* barcode
* brand
* category
* subcategory
* specification
* model
* HSN
* vendor
* vendor SKU
* aliases
* tags

Examples:

"2.5"

"2.5 wire"

"polycab"

"fan 1200"

"mcb 16"

"anchor switch"

"house wire"

Search should tolerate partial terms.

The search must be fast.

Do not load all products into the browser.

Use backend search with appropriate indexes/query optimization.

---

Each result should be able to show:

* product
* brand
* specification

* current/active batch
* purchase cost

* safe selling price
* maximum discount
* preferred vendor
* last purchase price
* lowest historical purchase price

Provide quick actions:

* View product
* Add to sale
* View price history
* View vendors
* View stock
* Adjust stock

# 6. INVENTORY BATCHES

Implement the batch architecture designed in Phase 1.

Every purchase that creates new stock must be capable of creating a batch.

A batch should record:

* product
* vendor
* purchase date
* invoice/reference
* quantity purchased
* remaining quantity
* purchase price
* supplier discount
* effective cost
* MRP
* selling price
* safe selling price
* maximum discount
* notes

Do not overwrite old batches.

---

# 7. NEW BATCH ENTRY

When an existing product is purchased again:

Detect the previous batch.

Show a compact notification:

"Previous batch detected."

Show:

Previous batch:

* vendor
* date
* purchase cost
* selling price
* safe selling price
* maximum discount
* remaining stock

New batch:

* purchase cost

Calculate:

* cost difference
* percentage change
* selling-price difference
* safe-price difference

Example:

Previous cost: ₹1,450
New cost: ₹1,580
Increase: ₹130
Increase %: 8.97%

---

# 8. OWNER-CONTROLLED PRICING

When entering a new batch, allow the owner to choose:

1. Use previous pricing
2. Copy previous pricing and edit
3. Set completely new pricing

The application must NEVER silently change the owner's pricing.

The owner must explicitly confirm pricing.

---

# 9. PURCHASE ENTRY PRICING FIELDS

The inventory/batch entry interface should capture:

Purchase price
Supplier discount %
Supplier discount amount
Effective purchase cost

MRP
Selling price
Minimum safe selling price
Maximum customer discount

Optional:

Bulk selling price
Customer-type pricing
Notes

Calculate and display:

* gross margin
* margin percentage
* discount impact

before saving.

---

# 10. SAFE SELLING PRICE

Safe selling price is explicitly entered or approved by the owner.

It must NOT be automatically imposed by the software.

The application can provide calculations/recommendations, but the owner controls the final value.

The value must be stored with the batch.

---

# 11. BATCH PRICE HISTORY

Build a complete history.

Example:

Product:
Polycab 2.5 sq mm wire

Batch #001
Cost ₹1,450
Selling ₹1,900
Safe ₹1,750
Stock 4

Batch #002
Cost ₹1,580
Selling ₹2,050
Safe ₹1,850
Stock 20

Never overwrite Batch #001 when Batch #002 is created.

# 15. INVENTORY VALUATION

Create inventory calculations using the selected valuation approach.

Do not hard-code one valuation method throughout the application.

Create a service architecture so FIFO/weighted-average/etc. can be supported.

For the initial implementation, document which valuation method is being used.
